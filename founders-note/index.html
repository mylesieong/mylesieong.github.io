The workspace that was going to remember your whole company.

Founder's Note was going to be the place where your notes, documents, tasks and progress fed one agent that already knew everything — so it could sit in calls, write outreach and build reports without being briefed each time. Founders agreed the problem was real. None of them wanted to pay to solve it, so it was stopped during ideation and no part of it was built.

The problem

The hardest part isn't ambition.

It's re-explaining your company every time.

📋

Strategy lives in scattered notes and threads

Strategy, customer learnings, and product progress never stay in one place.

📞

Meetings happen without memory in the room

Important calls start cold — nothing remembers what you already decided.

✉️

Outreach and decks are rebuilt from scratch

Generic AI is smart but amnesiac; your full story doesn't travel with you.

The mechanism

Build context once. Use it everywhere.

Notes, documents, tasks, and calendar feed one context-aware agent — not a menu of generic coaches.

What happened instead

Post-mortem

Killed products keep their URL here and gain this instead of a redirect.

What I believed
That founders were paying a real price for context switching. I was in an incubator at the time, watching people hold a dozen streams of information at once, and I assumed that if all of it lived in one place with a single agent on top, they would pay to stop paying that price.
What I built
Nothing. The pitch, the positioning and this page, and that is the whole list. It was killed during ideation, before a line of the product was written.
What the evidence said
Talking to founders through the ideation phase. The context-switching cost was real and they recognised it immediately — and none of them wanted to pay for it. The problem was not painful enough to be worth money. They were handling it badly and were entirely willing to keep handling it badly.
What I would do differently
Nothing about the kill itself. I had only spent time on the landing page, so stopping cost me almost nothing, and I would stop again. What I would change is the evidence I stopped on. I judged it from a handful of conversations with the two or three founders I could actually reach, and that is a sample small enough that “nobody wants this” and “I asked the wrong three people” look exactly the same from the inside. I should have put it in front of a much larger group first — posted it publicly, on social, let a few hundred people ignore it or not — before concluding on my own that it would not work.
